CASE AND AIRFLOW - notes
========================

the component people spend the least thought on and then fight for years.


PRESSURE BALANCE
----------------
three states, and you get to choose:

positive - intake moves more air than exhaust. surplus air leaves through every gap
and seam in the case. dust only enters where you put a filter. this is what i run.

negative - exhaust moves more than intake. the case sucks air in through every
unfiltered gap, seam, and empty pci bracket. dust everywhere, inside everything.
usually marginally better raw temperatures. not worth it.

neutral - theoretical. does not survive contact with a dirty filter.

practical implementation: intake fans slightly outnumber or out-spin exhaust fans.
i run three front intake, one rear exhaust. the imbalance is deliberate.

radek pokorny's point, which changed how i think about this: a filter you don't
clean is worse than no filter at all. a clogged front filter starves the intake,
which flips a positive-pressure case to negative, which pulls unfiltered dust in
through every gap. so the filter doesn't just stop working - it inverts the whole
strategy. clean them monthly. radek.pokorny@example.com if i need the fan model he
recommended.


FAN TYPES - not interchangeable
-------------------------------
high airflow fans: widely spaced blades, move a lot of air against little
resistance. correct for open case positions - a rear exhaust, a top exhaust, an
unobstructed side.

high static pressure fans: closer blades, designed to push against resistance.
correct for anything mounted to a radiator, a dense heatsink fin stack, or behind a
restrictive front panel with a dust filter.

putting an airflow fan on a radiator is the common error. the specs look better on
paper and the actual performance is worse, because the rated airflow assumes nothing
in the way.

fan size: bigger moves the same air at lower rpm, and rpm is what you hear. a 140mm
fan at 800rpm and a 120mm fan at 1100rpm move comparable air, and one of them is
audible. buy the biggest the case takes.


THE FRONT PANEL PROBLEM
-----------------------
a solid glass or metal front panel with a 10mm slot down each side looks good and
strangles the intake. no fan configuration recovers from a front panel that won't
pass air.

check before buying: can air actually get in? mesh front, or large perforated
sections. this single property affects temperatures more than the number of fan
mounts does.

learned this by owning one. swapped a case, kept every other component, gpu ran 8c
cooler.


CABLE MANAGEMENT IS AIRFLOW, NOT AESTHETICS
--------------------------------------------
a cable bundle across the front intake is a wall in front of the fans. behind the
motherboard tray it costs nothing. this is the actual reason to route cables, and
the aesthetic argument is secondary.

velcro over zip ties - you will open this machine again.


MEASURE BEFORE ORDERING
-----------------------
three numbers, every time, and i have got each of them wrong at least once:

1. gpu length vs case clearance. check whether the quoted clearance assumes the
   front fans or drive cage are removed - it usually does. mine needed the drive
   cage out, which i discovered with the card in my hand.
2. cpu cooler height vs side panel clearance. a tower cooler 2mm too tall means the
   panel does not close and there is no workaround.
3. memory heat spreader height vs cooler overhang. tall rgb memory fouls the front
   fan of many dual-tower coolers. some coolers let you raise that fan, which then
   reintroduces problem 2.

also worth checking: psu length vs the psu shroud, especially with a fully modular
supply where the cables need a bend radius behind it. and radiator thickness plus
fan thickness vs top clearance, because the motherboard vrm heatsink often sits
right where a thick top radiator wants to go.


GPU SAG
-------
heavy cards bend at the pci-e slot over months. it is a slow mechanical problem, not
a cosmetic one - the slot and the pcb both take load. a support bracket costs almost
nothing. fit it at build time rather than after noticing the droop.


WHAT I'D BUY AGAIN
------------------
mesh front, three 140mm intake, one 140mm rear exhaust, top vents left closed
because open top vents in a positive pressure case just let the surplus out where i
would rather it went through the gpu first. filters on all intakes. no side intake -
it fights the front-to-back path.

lucie simkova (lucie.simkova@example.com) runs the same case with a top exhaust and
argues i'm wrong about the top vents. she may be. i have not measured it properly.
