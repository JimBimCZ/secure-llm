RAM BUILD LOG - ddr5 memory, the boot loop saga
================================================

not a tidy document. dated entries, written as it happened. skip to 14/04 for the
actual answer if you're reading this to fix something.


02/04
-----
bought 2x16gb ddr5-6000 cl30, expo kit. plan is 32gb, two sticks only, leaving the
other two slots empty on purpose. reason: on am5, populating all four dimm slots
drops you to something like 3600-4400 mt/s stable, because the memory controller has
to drive twice as many loads. two sticks of 32gb beats four sticks of 16gb on this
platform basically always. worth writing down because it's counterintuitive - more
sticks is slower.

jedec base speed for ddr5 is 4800 mt/s. everything above that is an overclock, no
matter what the box says. expo (amd) and xmp (intel) are just stored overclock
profiles the board applies for you. they are not guaranteed. this becomes relevant
below.


03/04
-----
installed. posted first try. enabled expo in bios. did not post. no display, no
beep, debug led sitting on the yellow DRAM light.

cleared cmos, posted again at 4800. so the sticks are fine, the profile isn't
applying cleanly.


04/04
-----
tried the obvious things:
 - reseated both sticks. slots a2/b2 as the manual says (NOT a1/b1 - the far slots
   from the cpu are the correct pair for two-stick config, this is the opposite of
   what feels right)
 - updated bios. two versions behind. this alone fixed nothing but is a prerequisite
   for everything below
 - memory training takes 30-60s on first boot after a change on am5. i had been
   pulling the plug at ~20s assuming it had hung. it had not hung. it was training.
   this wasted two of the four days.

lesson: after any memory change, leave it alone for a full two minutes before
declaring it dead.


09/04
-----
expo applies now after the bios update but memtest throws errors after ~40 min.
so: posts, boots, runs, and is silently corrupting things. worse than not booting.

started reading about the 1:1 ratio. on am5 the memory clock and the memory
controller clock want to run 1:1. ddr5-6000 = 3000 mhz memclk, so uclk 3000, and
fclk somewhere around 2000. if the board decides to run 2:1 instead, latency goes up
badly and you lose most of what the faster kit bought you. above roughly 6400 mt/s
most chips can't hold 1:1 any more, which is why 6000 cl30 is the sweet spot people
keep repeating. it isn't the fastest kit, it's the fastest kit that stays in 1:1.

checked mine: it was at 1:1. so that wasn't it.


12/04
-----
qvl. the qualified vendor list. the board maker tests specific kits by part number
and publishes what worked. my kit was not on it. i had assumed "ddr5-6000 cl30" was
a specification. it is not - it's a marketing summary. the actual variable is which
memory die is inside, and vendors change dies mid-production without changing the
part number on the box.

petra horakova (petra.horakova@example.com) has the same board and pointed this out
in about four minutes, after i'd spent a week. her advice verbatim: "the kit part
number is the only thing that means anything, and only against your board's qvl."


14/04 - RESOLVED
----------------
raised vsoc slightly and loosened tras and trc a little from the expo values. stable
through 12 hours of memtest and a full night of prime95 large fft.

so the kit is fine, the expo profile was just marginally too tight for this
particular board + cpu memory controller combination. that's the actual lesson:
expo is a starting point, not a guarantee. the silicon lottery applies to the memory
controller in your cpu just as much as to the cores.

do NOT push vsoc past 1.3v on am5. this is a hard limit, not a suggestion - there
were failures early in the platform's life traced to boards feeding excessive soc
voltage. mine sits well under.


SUMMARY - what i'd tell someone starting
----------------------------------------
1. two sticks, not four, on am5. always.
2. slots a2/b2.
3. buy a kit that is on your board's qvl, by exact part number.
4. ddr5-6000 cl30 is the target unless you enjoy tuning. above that you fall out of
   1:1 and it's a wash.
5. after a memory change, wait two full minutes before assuming it's hung.
6. capacity beats speed for anything real. 32gb at 5600 is a better machine than
   16gb at 7200. the speed argument only matters once you have enough.
7. dual channel matters more than either. one 32gb stick is meaningfully worse than
   two 16gb sticks - roughly half the bandwidth to the memory controller.
